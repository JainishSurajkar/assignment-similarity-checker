A machine learning project usually includes data collection, model training,
and evaluation. The final report should describe the chosen method and the
measures used to assess its performance.

Different algorithms may produce different results on the same dataset.
Therefore, experiments should be documented carefully so that the findings
can be interpreted and reproduced.
Clustering organizes observations without requiring a target label. In
hierarchical clustering, documents begin as separate items and nearby items
are merged according to a distance rule. A dendrogram can be cut at a chosen
distance to obtain a practical set of groups.

For text documents, cosine distance between TF-IDF vectors is an interpretable
choice. Important terms can summarize a group, but a cluster threshold does
not guarantee that every pair inside a connected group is directly similar.
The silhouette score can describe separation when its mathematical
requirements are met; it is undefined for one cluster or for all singletons.

Clustering is exploratory. The resulting groups should be discussed with
subject knowledge rather than presented as objectively correct categories.
