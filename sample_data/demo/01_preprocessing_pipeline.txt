Data preprocessing is the stage where raw observations are converted into a
form that an algorithm can use. A practical pipeline can remove duplicate
rows, repair missing values, standardize numeric columns, and encode
categorical values. The order matters because a later transformation should
not accidentally use information that belongs only to a test split.

For an assignment similarity project, preprocessing also applies to text.
Line endings and unnecessary whitespace are normalized while blank lines remain
paragraph boundaries. The resulting paragraphs are converted to TF-IDF
vectors, so common words receive less weight than words that distinguish one
document from another.

The final model should be checked on data that was not used while fitting the
preprocessing steps. This separation makes the evaluation more credible,
although a small synthetic benchmark cannot establish performance on real
student work.
