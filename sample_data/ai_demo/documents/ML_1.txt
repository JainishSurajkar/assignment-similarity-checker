Machine learning is a field of artificial intelligence in which computer systems learn patterns from examples instead of relying only on fixed instructions. A model is trained using data and then applies what it has learned to make predictions or decisions about new observations. The quality of learning depends on the relevance, quantity, and reliability of the training data.

In supervised learning, each training example has an input and a known target. Classification predicts categories, such as whether an email is spam, while regression predicts numerical values, such as house prices. In unsupervised learning, examples do not have target labels. Clustering is one method for discovering groups of similar observations.

A machine learning workflow includes collecting data, preprocessing features, dividing data into training and testing portions, selecting an algorithm, fitting the model, and evaluating its performance. Accuracy alone may be misleading when classes are imbalanced. Precision, recall, F1-score, and a confusion matrix provide additional information for classification tasks.

Machine learning is applied in image recognition, medical decision support, recommendation systems, language processing, and predictive maintenance. Models can also overfit by memorising training examples and performing poorly on new data. Cross-validation, regularisation, appropriate feature selection, and independent testing help assess whether a model generalises.
